# User Guide (for everyone — no technical background needed)

CyberShield helps you check suspicious messages and links, learn how scams
work, and help your community track scam patterns. Everything is anonymous:
no accounts, no names, no personal details.

## 1. Analyze a suspicious message

1. Open **Check a Message** (home page → *Check a message*, or the *Check*
   tab at the bottom on mobile).
2. Paste the SMS, WhatsApp message or email text into the box.
3. Tap **Analyze message**.
4. Read the result: risk level, category, **why it was flagged**, and the
   **recommended action**. The score is guidance, not proof.

⚠️ Never paste passwords, OTPs, PINs, CVVs or card numbers.

## 2. Analyze a suspicious URL

1. Open **Check a URL**.
2. Paste the full link and tap **Analyze URL**.
3. Read the structural indicators (e.g. IP address host, misleading
   brand-like name) plus the limitation note: indicators alone do not
   prove a site is malicious — and the link was never visited.

## 3. Understand the result

- **Risk level** (Low/Medium/High) + plain-language category.
- **Why this was flagged**: the exact signals found, each explained.
- **Recommended action**: the safe next step (e.g. verify through the
  organisation's official app or helpline, never the message itself).
- **“How we assessed this”**: expand for method details and limitations.

## 4. Report a scam

1. Tap **Report this message/link** on a result, or open **Report** from
   the menu.
2. Check the pre-filled category, add an approximate locality (never your
   address) and optional description.
3. Submit. If the same content was already reported, your submission
   strengthens the existing signal instead of duplicating it.

## 5. View community trends

Open **Community**: total reports, this-week activity, top category and
risk mix — all aggregated and anonymous. *“No community reports yet”*
means exactly that: nothing submitted so far, not zero crime.

## 6. Learn scam-prevention concepts

Open **Learn**: short topics (what it is, warning signs, fictional
example, do / never-do), then **Practice** scenarios — choose what you
would do and read why it is safe or not.

## 7. Take the quiz

Open **Quiz**: answer all questions, submit once — scoring happens on the
server. Review per-question explanations plus **topics to review** picked
from your weakest categories.

## 8. Complete the awareness assessment

Open **Awareness**: answer 6 questions (**before**), study the material,
answer again (**after**). Your anonymous result shows how your quiz score
**changed** — a learning measure, not a safety certificate.
